Every name here has a fixed date on which something binary happens — a bid deadline, an FDA decision, a trial readout. Each card shows the price now, a reference price for each way the event can go, and when.

Price now is the live quote. If it works and If it fails are reference prices that already exist — an offer price, an undisturbed price, a 52-week extreme — never a forecast. Each says what it is, and each is marked contractual (a real outcome of this event, such as an offer against the pre-bid price) or indicative (a historical reference standing in for an outcome). Breakeven odds is computed from whichever two endpoints are in the boxes on the card — the defaults, or yours — and is worth exactly what those endpoints are worth. Your odds is the slider.

    Hard dates on names too large to move much on them, or without a verified price this run. Useful for the calendar, not for the sleeve.

      How the numbers are built

      Opportunity = asymmetry×0.35 + date clarity×0.20 + uncrowdedness×0.25 + reference quality×0.20, each 0–10, then ×10. Risk = illiquidity×0.20 + single point of failure×0.30 + downside to floor×0.30 + slip risk×0.20. High is bad.

      Four inputs are computed from the live price on every build, using the card's default endpoints: asymmetry (the gap to the upside reference against the gap to the downside one — equal gaps score 5), uncrowdedness (position in the 52-week range, less a penalty for a big one-month run), downside to floor (distance to the downside reference) and illiquidity (daily value traded). The other four are judgements made when the name was added and are shown as bars so you can disagree with one specifically.

      Endpoints. A pair of endpoints is contractual only when both are observable outcomes of the specific event — an offer price and the undisturbed price in a bid. Everything else is indicative: a 52-week high or low is a historical reference, not an outcome of a catalyst, and the offer-period high in a contested bid is a hope, not a price on the table. Indicative breakevens are marked and should be read as arithmetic on placeholders. On any card you can type your own endpoints; breakeven and the expected move follow them.

      Breakeven odds = downside% ÷ (upside% + downside%) from the two endpoints and the price now — the probability of the good outcome at which the expected move is zero. The slider's expected move is p × upside% − (1 − p) × downside%. Endpoints, odds and marks are saved in this browser only.

      Downside bias. Using a 52-week low as the failure case understates the downside for names that have already fallen, because their low sits close to the current price. Where a card is indicative and the price is in the bottom quarter of its 52-week range, the card says so and asks for your own floor rather than adjusting the number silently.

      A high opportunity score is not a recommendation. The highest-opportunity names here also carry the highest risk scores, and that is the honest shape of event-driven trading.
