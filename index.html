Event Screen

Dated, binary events over the next ten weeks, ranked. Every name has a fixed or guided date on which something resolves. Scores are computed from the four inputs shown on each card — open the methodology at the bottom to see the weights.

The levels are reference prices, not targets: an offer price, an undisturbed price, a net asset value, a 52-week extreme. Each is labelled with what it actually is. The screen gives you the payoffs; you supply the probability — that's the judgement, and it's the half worth having.

Sort Type

Dated, not scored

Hard dates on names too large to move much on them, or where I haven't verified a price this run. Useful for the calendar, not for the sleeve.

How the scores are built

Opportunity = asymmetry×0.35 + date clarity×0.20 + uncrowdedness×0.25 + reference quality×0.20, each input scored 0–10.

Asymmetry: the gap between the downside and upside reference relative to where it trades now. Date clarity: a Rule 2.6 deadline or a PDUFA date scores 10; "by end of October" scores 6. Uncrowdedness: position in the 52-week range and how much has already moved — a name up 400% into its catalyst scores near zero. Reference quality: whether there's a hard, computable anchor (an offer price, a published NDV) or only a soft one.

Risk = illiquidity×0.20 + single-point-of-failure×0.30 + downside×0.30 + slip risk×0.20. High is bad. A name resting on one FDA decision scores 10 on single-point-of-failure; one with three staggered readouts scores lower.

These are my judgements on a consistent scale, not measurements. The point of showing the four inputs is that you can disagree with one of them specifically rather than with a black-box number. A high opportunity score is not a recommendation — note that the two highest here also carry the two highest risk scores, which is the honest shape of event-driven trading.

Prices are snapshots from the timestamp above, not live. Nothing here is advice, and the ten-week window means every one of these resolves — up or down — inside your holding period.
