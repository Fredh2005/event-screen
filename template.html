Every name here has a fixed date on which something binary happens — a bid deadline, an FDA decision, a trial readout. The cards show the price you'd enter at today, the two prices you'd exit at depending on the result, and when.

Entry is simply the current price. Exit if it works and Exit if it fails are reference prices that exist independently — an offer price, an undisturbed price, a 52-week extreme — never a forecast. Each one says what it is. The page gives you the payoff either way. Breakeven odds is the one probability the page computes: how likely the good outcome has to be for entering at today's price to pay, given those two exits. Your odds is the slider — set what you believe and the card shows the expected move at your number.

How the scores are built

Opportunity = asymmetry×0.35 + date clarity×0.20 + uncrowdedness×0.25 + reference quality×0.20, each 0–10, then ×10.

Risk = illiquidity×0.20 + single point of failure×0.30 + downside to floor×0.30 + slip risk×0.20. High is bad.

Four inputs are computed from the live price on every build: asymmetry (the gap to the upside reference against the gap to the downside one — equal gaps score 5), uncrowdedness (position in the 52-week range, less a penalty for a big one-month run), downside to floor (distance to the downside reference) and illiquidity (daily value traded). The other four are judgements made when the name was added and are shown as bars so you can disagree with one specifically.

Breakeven odds = downside% ÷ (upside% + downside%), from the two exit references and the current price. It is the probability of the good outcome at which the expected move is zero — the number the price is implying, not a forecast. The slider's expected move is p × upside% − (1 − p) × downside%, saved in this browser only.

A high opportunity score is not a recommendation. The highest-opportunity names here also carry the highest risk scores, and that is the honest shape of event-driven trading.
